$\langle\cdot\rangle$ = translation-invariant cell average; admissible

\paragraph{Theorem.} If $r > r^* \equiv u^2/(4v)$, then $\phi = 0$ is the
ONLY stationary point of $F$ (no mean-field condensate of any form exists).
Numerically $r^* = 0.0570679$. In the production convention

\paragraph{Proof.} Write $\phi = t\,\psi$ with $\langle\psi^2\rangle = 1$,
$t \ge 0$. Then $F(t)/V = \tfrac{\kappa}{2} t^2 + \tfrac{u}{4} P_4 t^4
+ \tfrac{v}{6} P_6 t^6$ with $\kappa = \langle\psi K \psi\rangle \ge r$,
$P_4 = \langle\psi^4\rangle$, $P_6 = \langle\psi^6\rangle$. A stationary
$t > 0$ requires the quadratic (in $X = t^2$)
$v P_6 X^2 + u P_4 X + \kappa = 0$ to have a positive real root, hence
\[
u^2 P_4^2 \;\ge\; 4 \kappa v P_6 \;\ge\; 4 r v P_6 .
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz in the cell average, $\langle\psi^4\rangle =
\langle\psi\cdot\psi^3\rangle \le \langle\psi^2\rangle^{1/2}
\langle\psi^6\rangle^{1/2}$, gives $P_4^2 \le P_6$ (using
$\langle\psi^2\rangle = 1$). Therefore a stationary point requires
$u^2 \ge 4 r v$, i.e.\ $r \le u^2/(4v) = r^*$. Contrapositive: for
$r > r^*$ no nonzero stationary point exists. Equality $P_4^2 = P_6$ would
require $\psi^3 \propto \psi$, i.e.\ $\psi^2$ constant a.e., unattainable
for any non-constant real field; so the bound is strict on the relevant
class. $\square$
