\paragraph{Definition}. After subtracting the UV-cancelling contact terms, the finite residue is
\[
\frac{\Delta F_{\rm Brazov}^{\rm finite}(r_R)}{V} \;=\; \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{d^3 q}{(2\pi)^3} \biggl[\ln\!\bigl(r_R + \kappa (q^2 - q_*^2)^2\bigr) - L_{\rm sub}(q; \Lambda)\biggr],
\]
where $L_{\rm sub}(q; \Lambda) = \ln(\kappa\,q^4) + O(\Lambda^{-2})$ is the UV-cancelling subtraction (chosen so the integral is UV-finite). Because the subtraction is $r_R$-independent (it captures only the UV behaviour), the residue $\Delta F_{\rm Brazov}^{\rm finite}$ depends on $r_R$ alone (and on the locked $\kappa, q_*, u$).

\paragraph{Hartree closed-form (standard Brazovskii result)}. At one-loop in the standard Brazovskii treatment (Brazovskii 1975; Math400-AddE Path $\alpha$ adopted methodology), the finite residue is the standard one-loop free-energy of a quadratic theory with mass $r_R$:
\[
\frac{\Delta F_{\rm Brazov}^{\rm finite}(r_R)}{V} \;=\; \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{d^3 q}{(2\pi)^3} \ln \frac{r_R + \kappa (q^2 - q_*^2)^2}{\kappa\,q^4} \;\equiv\; f_1(r_R; \kappa, q_*),
\]
with $f_1$ finite + UV-cutoff-independent + $r_R$-monotone-increasing (standard Brazovskii thermodynamic identity).

\paragraph{Differential identity (key relation)}. The standard $\partial_m {\rm Tr}\ln(H + m^2) = {\rm Tr}\,G(m^2)$ identity gives:
\[
\frac{\partial f_1(r_R)}{\partial r_R} \;=\; \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{d^3 q}{(2\pi)^3} \frac{1}{r_R + \kappa (q^2 - q_*^2)^2} \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\,\langle |\Psi|^2 \rangle.

This identifies $\partial f_1 / \partial r_R$ with the half-fluctuation-density, a structural consequence of the 1-loop ${\rm Tr}\ln$ formula.

\paragraph{Brazovskii self-consistency $\to$ $r_R$ as function of $N/V$}. The self-consistency equation
\[
r_R \;=\; r_{\rm bare} + \frac{u}{2} \langle |\Psi|^2 \rangle \;=\; r_{\rm bare} + \frac{u}{2} \cdot \frac{N}{V}

\[
\delta\mu \;=\; \frac{\partial (\Delta F_{\rm Brazov}^{\rm finite}/V)}{\partial (N/V)} \;=\; \frac{\partial f_1(r_R)}{\partial r_R} \cdot \frac{\partial r_R}{\partial (N/V)} \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\langle |\Psi|^2 \rangle \cdot \frac{u}{2} \;=\; \frac{u}{4} \langle |\Psi|^2 \rangle.

This is the Hartree chemical-potential shift, finite, dimensionally consistent (energy units), and proportional to the fluctuation density $\langle |\Psi|^2 \rangle = N/V$.

\[
\Delta F_{\rm Brazov}^{\rm finite}/V \;=\; \int_0^{N/V} \delta\mu(N'/V)\,d(N'/V) \;=\; \int_0^{\langle |\Psi|^2 \rangle} \frac{u}{4}\,n\,dn \;=\; \frac{u}{8}\,\langle |\Psi|^2 \rangle^2,

i.e., the standard Hartree mean-field free-energy contribution. This is a chemical-potential-shift contribution, NOT a vacuum-energy contribution to $\Lambda$, by the standard thermodynamic interpretation:
\[

\paragraph{Objection $\beta$ (CONCRETE: TECT scalar field may not be exactly complex U(1) --- could be real, in which case the U(1) Noether-charge argument fails)}.

\paragraph{Objection $\gamma$ (CONCRETE: ``chemical-potential shift'' may itself contribute to the effective cosmological constant in cosmological context, where $\mu$ is dynamical and couples to gravity)}.

\textsc{Response: DISMISSED at standard QFT level}. In standard QFT, the chemical potential $\mu$ enters the action as a Lagrange multiplier for conserved charge $\mu N$; it does NOT couple to the metric directly. The cosmological constant $\Lambda$ couples to the metric via $\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ in the Einstein equations. A shift $\delta\mu$ modifies the thermodynamic ground-state energy at fixed $N$ but does NOT enter the Einstein equations as a $\Lambda$ contribution. This is the standard separation between (a) chemical-potential-controlled charge-density-dependent free-energy contributions and (b) absolute vacuum-energy $\Lambda$ contributions; Math58-v5 Step 4 (Reading F) and the present AddC (Reading H) both rest on this standard separation.

\textsc{Response: VALID-to-acknowledge}. The Brazovskii finite free energy IS a real physical quantity (it determines phase transition properties, equation of state, etc.).
